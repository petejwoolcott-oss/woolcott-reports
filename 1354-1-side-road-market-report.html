Why the "most likely sale price" is lower than the indicated value

Indicated market value assumes normal market conditions — a properly exposed listing where buyers see a fresh property and negotiate from a balanced position. With 563 days of accumulated market history, that's not the situation you're in. Every buyer who walks through your door has already been informed by their agent that this home has been listed for a year and a half without selling. That's leverage in their hands, not yours. The 563 CDOM creates an expectation of a discount, and achieving full market value under these conditions requires either a buyer who doesn't know the history (unlikely), or a buyer with a specific need — the right family, the right timing — who values your home uniquely enough to pay what it's worth.

The Honest Answer

Is $2,600,000 a Realistic Minimum?

The market data places the indicated value of your home at approximately $2,490,000–$2,600,000. So in theory, yes — $2,600,000 sits at the very edge of what the market supports. The highest recent sale in the area, 2577 No 8 Side Road, sold for $2,620,000 in December 2025. That property had a heated saltwater pool, conservation land setting, and was positioned as a true lifestyle estate — but it confirms that buyers exist at that level.

The challenge is not the intrinsic value of your home. The challenge is what 563 days of market exposure does to that value in a negotiation. Buyers and their agents are not going to pay full market value for a home they know has been sitting. They are going to start lower — often significantly lower — and expect you to meet them somewhere in the middle. Based on how CDOM affects buyer psychology and negotiating leverage in this price range, a more realistic range for an actual sale — under current conditions — is $2,400,000 to $2,500,000.

If $2,600,000 is your absolute floor, that is a decision only you can make. But you should make it with clear eyes: the market data does not currently support expecting $2.6M as a likely outcome, and holding firm at that number extends the risk of another expired listing and further CDOM accumulation.

Recommendations Going Forward

  • 1
    Have a frank conversation about your number. The data supports a realistic sale in the $2,400,000–$2,500,000 range. If your true bottom line is $2.6M, be prepared for the possibility that a sale at that number requires exceptional timing and the right buyer. If you can accept $2,450,000–$2,500,000, a transaction is achievable.
  • 2
    Reset the CDOM story with a meaningful re-launch, not just a re-list. Simply changing the listing number does little when 563 days of history remains attached to the address. A genuine reset requires new photography, fresh staging, a renewed marketing campaign, and — critically — a price that creates new energy. Buyers and agents need a reason to look again.
  • 3
    Lean into what makes this home genuinely unique. No other active or recently sold property in North Burlington Rural comes close to 7,563 sqft of finished space with 6 bedrooms and 4.5 garage spaces at this price. This is a niche product for a specific buyer — a multigenerational family, a buyer relocating from a larger market, or someone who specifically needs the scale. Marketing should be targeted at that audience, not the general rural estate buyer.
  • 4
    Consider an inground pool as a selling point gap — or price to reflect it. Every comp at or above $2,400,000 either has a pool or offers something exceptional in lieu. Your home does not have a pool. Buyers at this price point expect resort-style outdoor living. Either install a pool (not advisable during a sale process) or accept that the absence needs to be reflected in pricing expectations.
  • 5
    Time is not your ally at this price point. Each additional month of CDOM makes it harder to justify the premium. The active listings you are competing against — particularly 5590 Blind Line and 6306 McNiven — have also been sitting, and eventually one of them will sell or reduce. When that happens, it resets buyer expectations for the entire market segment. Moving decisively now — before further market shifts — is in your interest.
