4. The Agent's Argument — Strengths and Limitations

Valid Points Supporting Premium

  • Finished basement adds meaningful value not reflected in 2021 MLS listing
  • 12 pages of documented custom upgrades — a genuinely rare level of finish
  • Backing greenspace is a real premium (confirmed by Grindstone Way comps)
  • Custom homes on either side — cohesive, high-quality streetscape
  • 4-year-old build in excellent condition — no deferred maintenance
  • Heated floors, 96" doors, European entry, hardwood throughout are visible luxury cues
  • Well-maintained; easy to present and market quickly

Limitations in the $2.25M Case

  • All 7 agent-provided comps sold below $2M except one at 5,133 sqft
  • 116 Grindstone — same greenspace backing, executive build — started at $2,097,000 and sold at $1,840,000 after 58 DOM
  • Active subdivision listings (non-custom) referenced but not shared — buyer pool can see these too
  • 573 Parkside confidential is a land value attribution, not an improved sale — not a clean comparable
  • No comp in the $2M–$2.2M range with similar size and location exists in the provided data
  • $2,250,000 at ~2,900 sqft = ~$776/sqft — no sold comp in this area approaches that $/sqft
The 116 Grindstone Lesson: This is the most instructive comparable in the set. 116 Grindstone Way is an executive-built bungaloft with 2,830 sqft above grade plus a fully finished 1,512 sqft basement (4,342 sqft total), custom bar, rebuilt deck, stamped concrete driveway, and a ravine-backing lot in the same Waterdown market. It listed at $2,097,000 in December 2025, sat on the market for 58 days, was reduced to $1,895,000, and sold at $1,840,000. Its journey provides a direct data point on where the ceiling currently sits for premium Waterdown product.

311 Parkside is a genuinely exceptional home that warrants a premium over the broader Waterdown resale market. The combination of a custom build, documented 12-page upgrade package, greenspace backing, and excellent maintenance puts this property in a class of its own within the active subdivision inventory. The agent is correct that standard resale comparisons understate the value — the relevant peer group is custom-finished, premium product.

However, the market data places a ceiling. The most directly applicable comparable — 116 Grindstone Way, an executive build with greenspace backing and a larger total finished footprint — sold at $1,840,000 after starting at $2,097,000 and sitting for 58 days. The only sold comp above $2M is nearly twice the size of 311 Parkside. The agent's expected sale figure of $2,050,000 is defensible as an aspirational outcome with the right buyer — but it requires a list price that keeps the property within the range where buyers are active, not one that signals overconfidence and invites low offers after extended DOM.

A list price of $2,099,000–$2,149,000 positions the home at its genuine market ceiling, allows room to negotiate to $2,000,000–$2,050,000 without a damaging public price reduction, and avoids the stigma of a $200,000 cut visible to every buyer's agent. The $2,250,000 entry point asks the seller to find a buyer willing to pay a $200,000 premium above what every recent comparable supports — possible, but the cost of not finding that buyer is weeks of market exposure and a very public narrative of overpricing.
The Risk of $2,250,000: When a home lists significantly above market and then reduces, buyers interpret the reduction not as a correction but as a signal that the seller is motivated — and they offer accordingly. The goal of achieving $2,050,000 is best served by a list price that brings multiple buyers into competition, not one that filters the buyer pool down to a single speculative offer.
A Note on the 573 Parkside Confidential: The agent notes that a neighbouring property (century home, needing upgrades) was assigned a value of approximately $2,355,000. This appears to represent land value or assessed value for the site, not the sale price of an improved property in comparable condition. Land value attributions on larger or development-potential lots on Parkside Drive are not directly transferable to the market value of a finished custom home, and should not be used as a pricing anchor without understanding the lot size and full context of the assignment.
