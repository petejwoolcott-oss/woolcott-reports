Market Read
What the Data Is Saying

This is a tight, self-referential dataset. All six properties are in the same complex, managed under the same condominium corporation, with the same $203/month fee structure. That makes it unusually clean for valuation purposes: the variables are condition, size, and timing, not neighbourhood drift or lot differences.

The three sales matching your exact 1,531 sq.ft. footprint landed between $585,000 and $620,500. The spread is meaningful. The high end belongs to Unit #67 in May 2025, which had undergone visible updates in 2024 and sold in seven days at $405/sq.ft. The low end belongs to Unit #45 in September 2024, a standard-condition home that sold at $382/sq.ft. in 43 days. The difference between the two results is condition, not market. Both were priced responsibly and sold within a reasonable window.

The two larger units (1,676 sq.ft.) tell a supporting story. Unit #38 sold for $595,000 in February 2026 after being correctly repositioned from a failed attempt at nearly $670,000. Unit #69 went firm in June 2026 at $652,500 after 90 days, reflecting both its end-unit advantage and an updated kitchen. Those results confirm the building's ceiling for the larger footprint under current market conditions.

Unit #65 is the one live data point. At 57 days on the market with a price reduction already in place, it is telling you something about where buyers are drawing a line right now for this building. The $640,000 range is not moving quickly, even with updates. That context matters when reading the sold comps alongside the active competition.

One practical factor worth noting: units with showing restrictions took longer to sell. Unit #69's 90-day runway included a four-hours-notice requirement. Buyer access directly shapes how quickly a home attracts a firm offer, and that consideration is specific to how a unit is occupied at the time it comes to market.

The renovated outlier, Unit #64 at $710,000, is a useful ceiling rather than a comp. It required a complete renovation to reach that result, and it represents the building's upper limit for buyers who are willing to pay for turnkey, high-end finishes. It is not a reference point for a standard-condition home.
