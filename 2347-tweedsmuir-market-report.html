Why It Hasn't Sold: Diagnostic Analysis

6 Contributing Factors Identified
① Overpriced From Day One

The property launched at $1,489,900 in November 2025 — $159K above the highest comparable sale in Brant Hills ($1,330,000 at 2378 Arnold Crescent). Even accounting for a full renovation premium, market evidence supports a value of approximately $1,348,000. Every price reduction since has been reactive, not strategic, leaving buyers to wonder how far it will fall rather than acting on current value.

Current price still ~$70K above market evidence
② 168 CDOM Is Visible To Every Buyer

Despite the April 30 re-list, every MLS sheet shows CDOM: 168. Buyers and buyer agents pull this data immediately. In a market where well-priced Brant Hills homes sell in 7–28 days, 168 CDOM creates a powerful "what's wrong with this house?" perception that suppresses showing traffic and offer activity regardless of price.

CDOM stigma cannot be hidden — only overcome with price
③ The Collapsed Conditional Deal

A buyer went conditional November 27, 2025 — deal fell through and property returned to market December 11. This is the single most damaging event in the listing history. Every buyer's agent researching this property sees it. The questions it raises: Did an inspection reveal issues? Did an appraisal come in short? Either scenario is concerning, and neither has been addressed publicly.

⚠ Proactive disclosure / inspection report recommended
④ Corporate Seller = Documentation Expectation

Seller is listed as "1964878 Ontario Inc." — a numbered company. While the renovation quality and finishes are self-evident in person, buyers purchasing from a corporate seller have a higher expectation for supporting documentation: permits, ESA electrical certificate, and a pre-listing home inspection. In the absence of these, some buyers hesitate or condition offers more aggressively. Proactively providing these materials removes friction at the offer stage.

⚠ Provide permits, ESA cert + inspection report upfront
⑤ Head-to-Head Competition at Lower Price

2391 Arnold Crescent — a 2-storey, 2,151 sqft, 4-bedroom home in the same Brant Hills neighbourhood — is currently listed at $1,279,900 with only 12 CDOM. Buyers comparing the two properties see a $120K premium for the subject, plus a 156-day DOM history. At that spread, buyers choose 2391 Arnold. Subject needs to close the gap or undercut to generate urgency.

$120K gap vs comparable active competition
⑥ Lot Size at Premium Price Point

At 40×94 ft (irregular), the subject has the smallest lot of any comparable in the active or sold set. Competing homes sit on 41×145, 45×120, 50×115, and 55×140 lots — substantially larger. Brant Hills buyers at this price point typically expect more outdoor space. The lot constraint is not a deal-breaker but it requires the price to compensate, not command a premium.

Seller Takeaways & Recommended Strategy

01
Reduce to $1,329,900 — Decisive and Final
Three price reductions and a re-list have not worked because none moved the price to where the market evidence sits. At $1,329,900 — just below the market ceiling established by 2378 Arnold ($1,330,000) — the subject is priced to reflect its genuine value: a complete gut renovation in a quiet court location with 2,160 sqft, 4+1 beds, and a 2-car garage. This positions the subject $50K above 2391 Arnold, a premium buyers can validate the moment they walk through the door.
02
Address the Collapsed Conditional Proactively
Every buyer agent who pulls this property's history sees the November conditional that fell apart. The most effective way to neutralize this is to make a pre-listing home inspection report available to prospective buyers immediately upon showing. If an ESA electrical certificate was obtained (as the comp at 2392 Arnold provided), make that available too. Transparency converts a red flag into a confidence builder.
03
Professional Staging Is Non-Negotiable
The property is vacant — which is excellent for showing flexibility but damaging for emotional connection. Buyers viewing a vacant gut-renovated home by a numbered company feel like they're walking through a product, not a potential home. Professional staging of the primary rooms (living/dining, family room, primary bedroom) will dramatically improve offer conversion. The renovation quality is real; staging lets buyers feel it.
04
Court Location Is a Genuine Strength — Lead With It
Tweedsmuir Court is a cul-de-sac — quiet, low traffic, safe for children, with a neighbourhood feel that family buyers in Brant Hills actively seek. This feature is undersold in the current listing narrative. Steps from Bruce T. Lindley Public School (French Immersion) adds significant family buyer appeal. Reframe the listing story around community and lifestyle, not just finishes.
05
Fresh Photography and New Listing Narrative
The same photos and listing copy that have been associated with 168 CDOM of market history need to be replaced entirely. New photography (ideally with staging in place), a rewritten narrative that emphasizes the court, the school proximity, the full 5-bedroom/4-bathroom configuration, and the renovation scope will present a meaningfully different product to the market — even if the underlying property is the same.
06
Expected Outcome at $1,329,900
At $1,329,900, SP/LP ratios of 0.97–0.99 point to a close range of $1,285,000–$1,320,000. The renovation quality is evident in person and fully supports the price relative to the comp set. With documentation provided upfront, staging in place, and fresh photography, this property has everything needed to close at the high end of its range. Pricing and perception have prevented the sale — the product has not.
