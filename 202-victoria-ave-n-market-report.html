The Subject Property

What We're Pricing

202 Victoria Avenue N is a circa-1900, 2.5-storey solid brick Victorian in Lansdale, legally assessed as a duplex and currently operating as two tenanted units. The home was substantially renovated in 2020–21 — new roof, A/C, 200-amp panel, flooring, both kitchens and baths — and features the expansive third-floor loft with exposed brick and skylight that defines this house type. One factor to weigh throughout this analysis is location: the property fronts onto Victoria Avenue N, a busier arterial road, which is noted in the MPAC assessment record and reflected in nearby sale prices.

The Duplex Economics

Both units are tenanted at rents that are at or near market. This is a real strength for marketing — the property arrives as a turnkey income producer or an ideal house-hack — and the income figures also help define where pricing works best.

At a $699,000 list, the gross rent multiplier is approximately 15.3× ($679,000 → 14.9×). Investors in this market typically start sharpening pencils in the 14–15× range; above 16× the cash-flow math stops working at current financing costs. In practical terms, the income comfortably supports a price in the high-$600s and keeps the widest pool of investor buyers engaged; above $750,000 the multiplier climbs past 16.4× and investor interest naturally narrows. The owner-occupier buyer paying a premium for size and Victorian character has also tended to favour the single-family format (see 483 King William below).

Closed & Pending Sales

What Buyers Actually Paid

Six comparable 2–2.5 storey century homes have transacted in Hamilton Centre over the past year. Not one sold at or above list — the average came in at 96.3% of asking after roughly 60 days on market. Two sold quickly (8–9 days), and both were priced at $749,900 in quieter neighbourhoods. The pattern is consistent: in this segment, well-calibrated pricing generates the fastest and strongest results.

Valuation & Pricing Strategy

Where the Numbers Converge

Three independent methods triangulate to the same answer. The price-per-square-foot band for larger century homes in this district runs $276–$339 (189 West to 176 Emerald, with 483 King William at $322 as the renovated ceiling). Applying a mid-band $310–$330 to the subject's 2,120 sq ft yields $657,000–$700,000. The HPI adjustment from last year's opinion lands at ~$682,000. And the income approach supports investor participation up to roughly $700,000 before the gross rent multiplier deteriorates past 15.5×.

The Bottom Line

Key Takeaways

  • Fair value is $660,000–$700,000; list at $679,000–$699,000. Price-per-square-foot analysis ($657K–$700K), the HPI adjustment (~$682K), and the income approach all converge on this window independently.
  • No comparable sold at asking. Six sales, average 96.3% of list, average 60 days on market. The two fastest sales (8–9 days) were the two priced most sharply — while 189 West Ave N needed 211 days and a re-list to reach $715,000 from a $774,990 start. Pricing accuracy at launch is the single biggest driver of outcome in this segment.
  • 483 King William ($770,000) sets the ceiling for this house type. The only matching-size sale in a year was a superior single-family renovation on a quieter street with a stronger primary suite — and even it took 51 days and a $30K adjustment. The subject is best positioned at a sensible margin below it.
  • The street factor is consistent and measurable. 187 Victoria Ave N — same street, three parking spaces, double garage — sold at $625,000. Victoria Avenue N addresses trade at a modest discount to quiet-street equivalents, and pricing that accounts for it up front supports a faster, cleaner sale.
  • The actives make $679–699K a clear positioning advantage. Every income property currently listed asks $749,000–$849,000. At the recommended band, the subject becomes the most affordable way to buy tenanted income in Hamilton Centre by roughly $100,000 — with $45,600/yr already in place at a workable ~15× multiplier.
  • Today's market is a different one from May 2022. The $945,000 purchase reflected the peak of an exceptionally competitive period — a 5-day sale at 135% of list. Current evidence across all three valuation methods sits well below that figure, and no pricing strategy can recreate those conditions. Pricing to today's market, rather than to 2022, gives the property its best chance at a strong, timely result.
