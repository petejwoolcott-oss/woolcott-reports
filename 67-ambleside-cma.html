Due Diligence

PRE-LISTING ACTION ITEMS

Four items should be addressed before going to market. Two are hard stops — they will appear in every offer negotiation if not resolved first.

Hard Stop Instrument LT114385 — Title Encumbrance

The geo report flags a registered instrument (LT114385) against title. Its nature — easement, restriction, or right-of-way — is not detailed in the public record. A buyer's lawyer will catch this on requisition. Get a title search done before listing so you can answer the question proactively. If it's a drainage or utility easement related to the ravine, it's likely routine — but confirm it.

Hard Stop Retaining Wall — Bottom of Property

A potential issue with the retaining wall at the rear was noted. This will come up in every home inspection on a ravine-backing property. Cost can range from $5,000 to $50,000+. Get a landscaping or structural contractor to assess it before listing. If the repair is minor, price it in. If it's significant, price accordingly and disclose. Don't let buyers discover this at inspection — it kills deals.

Recommended Non-Functioning Jacuzzi — Primary Ensuite

Disclose as-is at minimum. Consider removing the non-functional jacuzzi and converting to a standard soaker tub — a $2,000–$4,000 fix that eliminates a recurring negotiation point and presents the ensuite more cleanly for showings. At this price point, buyers will notice it.

Recommended "Abuts Commercial" — MPAC Notation

MPAC records show the property "abuts commercial" land. This almost certainly refers to the university-owned institutional land at the rear, which MPAC classifies commercially. Confirm this is the ravine/forest parcel and not an actual commercial property nearby. Being able to state definitively "backs onto Western University forest — classified institutional, zero development risk" turns a potential concern into a selling point.
