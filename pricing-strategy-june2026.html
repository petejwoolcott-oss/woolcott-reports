Why overpriced homes sell for less

Buyers use Days on Market as a signal. A home sitting 60+ days triggers the question: "What's wrong with it?" — even when the only problem is the price. Sellers end up negotiating from a position of weakness.

  • Week 1–2: Maximum Buyer Attention Every serious buyer in your price range sees a new listing immediately. This is your peak moment. Overpricing means you miss the buyers who are most motivated right now.
  • Week 3–4: Activity Drops Sharply Showings decline. Buyers have seen it, compared it to properly priced homes, and moved on. Price reductions at this stage recover some interest — but not all.
  • Day 45+: The Stigma Kicks In At 45+ days, buyers assume something is wrong — foundation, inspection, neighbours, or neighbourhood issues. You now need to overcome objections that have nothing to do with reality.
  • The Final Sale Price Is Often Lower Research consistently shows that homes requiring price reductions sell for less than they would have at the right price from day one. The longer it sits, the deeper the final discount.

The 3-Price Method

Every Home Has Three Prices.
Which One Matches Your Goal?

When we talk about pricing strategy, there are really only three scenarios. Understanding each one helps sellers make a clear, informed decision — not an emotional one.

Aggressive Price
Below Market
Typical result: Under 14 days

Drives immediate multiple offers. Best when you need certainty of sale or speed. Often results in final sale price at or above market due to competition. Works well in micro-pockets with tight inventory.

Best for: Sellers prioritizing speed, certainty, and maximum competition
Market Price
Right at Market
Typical result: 14–35 days

Attracts qualified buyers immediately. Solid negotiating position. Normal DOM for the area. Most sellers end up here — it balances maximum exposure with a strong final price.

Best for: Sellers who want maximum net proceeds with reasonable timing
Aspirational Price
Above Market
Typical result: 60+ days

Eliminates most qualified buyers on day one. Triggers "what's wrong with it?" stigma after 45 days. Usually ends in price reduction — often below where an accurate price would have started.

Warning: The market will eventually find fair value — but the journey is costly
